01 Rationale

Why Curating Your Information Intake Is a Deliberate Act

Every media diet is an architecture of attention. Most people inherit theirs from algorithms, geography and habit. The feeds they scroll and the newsletters that pile up were not chosen so much as accumulated, shaped by recommendation systems whose incentive is engagement, not understanding.

This project starts from the premise that how you read matters as much as what you read. A deliberately built ecosystem is not a luxury for the overinformed. It is a precondition for thinking clearly about a complex world, and an act of intellectual self governance.

The architecture serves explicit commitments: verification over virality, depth over speed, pluralism over comfort, and global coverage that does not shrink the world to a handful of capitals. Wire services set the factual baseline. Investigative nonprofits hold power to account without partisan capture. Specialist outlets go deeper than any generalist can. Research and official sources let the reader check claims against evidence instead of trusting secondhand summaries.

This is not a ranking of the best media in the world. The scores measure fit with one reader's needs, and the method is published so that anyone can adapt it. Treated this way, information literacy becomes a practice rather than a credential: conscious, structured, multilingual and open to revision.

02 Reader Profile

The Reader This Ecosystem Was Built For

A multilingual humanitarian cartographer based in Montreal, working at the intersection of geospatial analysis, disaster risk and open data, trained in the Swiss tradition where architecture, landscape architecture, urbanism and geography are taught together.

Trust Comes First

Outlets with published editorial standards, visible corrections and disclosed ownership set the baseline. Wire services and public broadcasters establish what happened; everything else is read against them.

Geographic Priorities

Canada and Quebec daily, Taiwan and the Traditional Chinese world in depth, then Switzerland, the United Kingdom and France, Japan, Southeast Asia, Central Asia and Russia, North and West Africa, Barbados and the Caribbean, Brazil and Latin America, and Indigenous and Arctic peoples.

Priority Themes

Hazard science, disaster risk reduction and resilience, humanitarian systems, mapping and symbolic systems, urban planning, landscape architecture and architecture, AI and technology, labour and SDG 8, climate, public policy, global political economy, arts and digital humanities.

Portfolio Balance

About 40 percent institutional anchors, 40 percent independent, specialist and nonprofit outlets, and 20 percent research and official sources, so that no single model of journalism dominates.

Five Working Languages

English and French every day. Traditional Chinese for depth on Taiwan. Japanese at an intermediate level with learner friendly news. Spanish occasionally at about B1. Arabic and Russian through translation and multilingual services.

No Sensationalism

Engagement bait, verification light content and false balance are filtered out. The ecosystem rewards evidence, method and nuance, and it represents competing views fairly without collapsing into empty centrism.

03 Methodology

How Every Source Was Scored

Each source receives up to 100 points across eight weighted criteria tailored to this profile, then a portfolio modifier of up to 15 points in either direction that judges how much it strengthens the ecosystem as a whole.

Base Score: Eight Weighted Criteria

Final score = Trust + Regional Depth + Niche Depth + Pluralism + Theme Fit + Language + Signal + Format + Portfolio Modifier, kept between 0 and 100.

Portfolio Modifier: Plus or Minus 15 Points

Points Added

When a source covers an underserved geography such as Barbados, the Sahel, Central Asia or the Arctic; fills a disciplinary gap such as landscape architecture, hazard science or cartographic history; or adds ownership diversity through a nonprofit, cooperative, collective, academic or reader funded model.

Points Removed

When a source is largely redundant with a stronger outlet already in the directory, adds prestige without perspective, has closed or survives only as an archive, or simply aggregates other outlets' work.

Worked Example

From Intake Form to Verified Directory

Information Intake Form

The reader answered thirty questions on purpose, geography, languages, time, topics, formats, ownership and blind spots. The answers became the specification for every later step.

Perplexity AI Deep Research

The profile was submitted to Perplexity's Deep Research mode, which returned a citation grounded set of outlets organized by region and topic, favouring outlets with published editorial standards.

ChatGPT Extended Thinking Deep Research

A second, independent pass with ChatGPT's Deep Research mode added niche journals, digital native outlets and sources outside English that the first pass missed.

Claude Opus 4.6 Extended: First Synthesis

Claude merged the research reports with the reader's own reading lists into a first scored directory.

Claude Opus 5.5: Audit and New Ranking

A full audit restored 50 sources that the first deduplication had dropped, merged true duplicates, separated Research from Ideas, added hazard science, disaster risk, urban planning and landscape architecture to the priority themes, and rescored every source on all eight criteria plus the modifier.

Live Verification

Every link was requested from an open network on 1 October 2026. Sites behind bot protection were confirmed through web archive captures and Wikidata's record of official websites. Closed outlets are labelled and linked to what survives of them.
